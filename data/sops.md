# Standard Operating Procedures

San Andreas First Responders (SAFR) handles emergency medical care across the state: street calls, hospital care at Pillbox, Paleto and Sandy Shores, and air rescue. This document is how we work. Read it before your first shift and come back to it when you're unsure.

> **Skyline is a semi-serious roleplay server.** We want realistic, believable medical RP, but nobody expects you to be a real paramedic. If you're not sure how something works, roleplay it calmly and confidently, then look it up here after the call.

## Code of Conduct

**On duty you represent SAFR.** Players judge the whole department by how you act on a scene.

- **Stay neutral.** We treat everyone: civilians, police, criminals, rivals. You never pick sides, carry messages for a crew, or hide evidence for anyone.
- **No crime on duty.** If you want to do crime, clock out, change out of uniform, and leave department vehicles and supplies behind.
- **No weapons on duty.** SAFR members don't carry firearms while clocked in. Your job is to save lives, not take them.
- **Scene safety first.** If shots were fired or a scene isn't secure, stage nearby and wait for police to call it **Code 4** before you go in. A downed medic helps nobody.
- **Wear the uniform.** Clock in only in a SAFR uniform for your rank (see the Clothing tab), and only when you're actually available for calls.
- **Professional radio.** Keep it short and in character. No music, no arguing, no inside jokes on the main channel.
- **Respect the patient's RP.** Let people play out their injuries. Don't rush revives just to clear a call, and don't revive someone who's mid-scene with police unless the officers are done with them.
- **No farming.** Treating friends over and over for pay, or staging fake injuries, is a removal-level offense.
- **Keep OOC out of RP.** If something goes wrong out of character, finish the scene and take it to a ticket. Never break a scene to argue.
- **Medical privacy.** Don't share a patient's treatment or identity with police unless they're on scene, the patient agrees, or command tells you to. Command can change this rule.

### Discipline

Breaking SOP usually goes: verbal warning, written warning, suspension, then removal. Command can skip steps for serious issues like farming, crime on duty or harassment.

## Ranks and Chain of Command

Orders flow down the chain. If two members disagree on a scene, the highest rank present makes the call. Take complaints about a decision to command after the scene, not during it.

| Grade | Rank | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Chief of Medicine | Runs the department. Final say on policy, hiring and removals. |
| 9 | Department Chief | Day-to-day leadership. Promotions, discipline and scheduling. |
| 4 to 8 | Paramedic (senior) | Advanced care, scene command, field training, supervisor vehicles. |
| 3 | Paramedic | Advanced life support: IV medications, pain management, critical patients. |
| 2 | AEMT | Advanced EMT: IV fluids and a few extra medications. |
| 1 | EMT | Basic life support. Drives the ambulance and handles most calls. |
| 0 | Recruit | In training. Always rides with an EMT or above. |

> **Recruits:** you never respond to a call alone. Ride with a field training officer or any member ranked EMT or higher until you're signed off.

## Responding to a Call

1. **Acknowledge** the call on the radio (10-4) and say you're en route (10-76).
2. **Drive safely.** Code 3 only for life-threatening calls. You can't help anyone if you crash on the way.
3. **Check the scene.** Shots fired or active fight? Stage a block away and wait for PD to call Code 4.
4. **Arrive** and call 10-23.
5. **Assess** the patient with a primary survey (ABCDE, below).
6. **Treat** what you find, worst injuries first.
7. **Transport** if they need the hospital. Call in the priority and your ETA.
8. **Hand off** at the hospital: who they are, what happened, what you found, what you did.
9. **Chart it.** Finish and sign the patient chart (see How srp_ems Works).
10. **Clear** the call and go back in service (10-8).

## Talking to Patients

How you talk sells the RP. Stay calm, introduce yourself and explain what you're doing.

- "Hi, I'm with San Andreas First Responders. Can you tell me your name?"
- "Do you know where you are right now? What day is it?"
- "Where does it hurt? On a scale of 1 to 10, how bad is it?"
- "Are you allergic to any medications?"
- "I'm going to check your arm now. You'll feel some pressure."
- "You're doing great. We're taking you to Pillbox to get you fixed up."

### Assessment terms

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| ABCDE | Primary survey order: Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability (brain and nerves), Exposure (check the whole body for injuries) |
| AVPU | Quick consciousness check: Alert, responds to Voice, responds to Pain, Unresponsive |
| LOC | Level of consciousness, or loss of consciousness ("Did they lose consciousness?") |
| GCS | Glasgow Coma Scale, a 3 to 15 consciousness score. 15 is fully awake, 3 is unresponsive |
| Vitals | Heart rate, blood pressure, breathing rate, oxygen level (SpO2), temperature |
| Chief complaint | The main problem in the patient's words ("My chest hurts") |
| MOI | Mechanism of injury: what caused it (fall, car crash, gunshot) |
| SAMPLE | History questions: **S**igns and symptoms, **A**llergies, **M**edications, **P**ast medical history, **L**ast oral intake (last meal or drink), **E**vents leading up |
| OPQRST | Pain questions: **O**nset (when did it start?), **P**rovocation (what makes it better or worse?), **Q**uality (sharp, dull, burning?), **R**egion and radiation (where is it, does it spread?), **S**everity (1 to 10), **T**ime (constant or comes and goes?) |
| Triage | Sorting several patients by how urgent they are |

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| GSW | Gunshot wound |
| MVA / MVC | Motor vehicle accident or collision |
| Laceration | A cut or tear in the skin |

## How srp_ems Works

### When you're hurt (everyone)

- Every hit lands on a body part (head, torso, arms, legs) as a gunshot, laceration, fracture, burn or blunt trauma.
- Leg injuries under 50% make you limp and stop you sprinting. A bad head injury blurs your vision now and then.
- At 0 HP you go into **last stand** for 60 seconds. You can crawl: **W** forward, **A / D** to turn. Taking more damage puts you flat right away.
- Then you go **flat**. You can't move, talk, or use your inventory or phone.
- Press **G** to send a distress signal to on-duty EMS (once a minute). It shows on their tablet and map.
- Respawning unlocks after **3 minutes** with no EMS on duty, or **6 minutes** when EMS are on. Hold **E** to wake up in the nearest hospital bed and pay a **$2,000** bill.
- Logging out while down doesn't save you. You come back down.

> **Don't respawn while a medic is on the way.** If EMS have answered your distress call, wait for them. Respawning on purpose to dodge RP can be treated as a rule break.

### Treating a patient (EMS on duty)

1. Third eye the patient and choose **Examine Patient**.
2. Downed patients get a top-down view. Standing patients get a face-to-face view and are held still while you work.
3. Drag a supply from your tray onto the injured body part. Each one only treats certain injuries:

| Supply | Treats |
|---|---|
| Bandage | Lacerations, blunt trauma |
| Hemostatic Gauze | Gunshot wounds, lacerations, bleeding |
| Splint | Fractures |
| Burn Gel | Burns |
| Trauma Kit | Gunshot wounds, fractures, blunt trauma (serious wounds) |
| Health Kit | Anything, but weaker |

4. Watch the vitals monitor (heart rate, blood pressure, SpO2, blood loss). It reacts as you treat.
5. When every body part reaches 100%, a downed patient **gets up revived** and a standing patient is healed to full.
6. Press **Finish & sign chart**, add notes and save. The patient's last 8 visits show up next time anyone examines them.

Higher ranks treat faster. You're paid **$150 per treatment** and **$750 per revive**, up to **$2,000 per patient per hour**.

### At the hospital

- **Check In** with the front desk doctor: $1,500, or $3,000 while EMS are on duty. Use this when no medic is around.
- Bring a downed player to the desk doctor and choose **Check In Patient**. They pay the bill.
- **Pillbox points** (third eye): Toggle Duty, Uniforms, Personal Locker, Medical Supplies (free), the garage in the basement, and the helipad on the roof.
- **Elevator:** third eye the doors to pick Main Floor, Basement or Helipad.

## Using the Gurney

All gurney options are through third eye, and only for on-duty EMS.

| Action | How |
|---|---|
| Get a gurney | Third eye the back doors of an ambulance and choose **Take out gurney**, or use the gurney spot inside Pillbox |
| Put a patient on | Third eye the **patient** (not the gurney) and choose **Put on gurney**. A gurney has to be within a few meters. Awake patients get asked first; downed patients go straight on |
| Push | Third eye the gurney, choose **Push gurney**. Press **E** to let go. You can't sprint or jump while pushing |
| Raise / lower | Third eye the gurney, choose **Raise gurney** or **Lower gurney** |
| Load into the ambulance | Push it to the back doors, third eye the gurney, choose **Load into ambulance**. The patient stays on it |
| Unload | Third eye the ambulance's back doors, choose **Unload gurney** |
| Take the patient off | Third eye the gurney, choose **Take patient off** |
| Put it away | Empty gurney only: third eye it near its ambulance or the Pillbox spot, choose **Put gurney away** |

**On the gurney (patient):** press **F** to get off if you're awake and the gurney isn't moving or loaded. Downed patients stay on until a medic takes them off or they're revived.

## Roleplay Tips

- **Narrate.** Use `/me` for what you do ("/me presses gauze firmly onto the wound") and `/do` for what others see ("/do The bleeding slows").
- **Slow down.** A two-minute scene with real questions beats a ten-second revive every time.
- **Ask what happened.** Patients love telling their story, and it gives police RP later.
- **Follow up.** Suggest a check-up, physical therapy or a cast. Hospital RP is as fun as street calls.
- **Transport serious patients.** Gunshots, big crashes and long falls go to Pillbox, even if you could treat them on scene.
- **Mind the crowd.** Ask bystanders to step back, and give police room to work.
- **Off duty is off duty.** Your character can have a life outside SAFR. Just don't use SAFR gear for it.
